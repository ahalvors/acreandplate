Cattle Breeds Raised for Beef

A general overview of common breeds you'll encounter when buying ranch-direct beef.

When you're buying beef directly from ranches, you'll see many different cattle breeds. Each has its own history and characteristics. Here's a quick reference for twelve breeds commonly raised for beef in the United States.

Keep in Mind: Coat color, size, marbling, and eating quality vary by individual herd, genetics, feed, and ranch management. This is a general overview—not a guarantee of what you'll get from any specific ranch.

Angus

Origin: Scotland
Known for: Consistent marbling and beef quality. Black coat is the most common; Red Angus is a color variant. Popular for both purebred and crossbreeding programs.

Charolais

Origin: France
Known for: Large, muscular frame with lean beef. Cream or white coat. Often crossed with other breeds to add size and yield.

Hereford

Origin: England
Known for: Hardy, adaptable cattle with a distinctive red body and white face. Moderate marbling and efficient foraging.

Limousin

Origin: France
Known for: Lean, tender beef with fine grain. Golden-red coat. Valued for muscle growth and minimal waste.

Brangus

Origin: United States (Brahman × Angus cross)
Known for: Heat tolerance from Brahman genetics combined with Angus marbling. Developed for southern climates.

Beefmaster

Origin: United States (composite of Brahman, Hereford, Shorthorn)
Known for: Efficiency and adaptability. Bred for practical performance in varied environments.

Simmental

Origin: Switzerland and Germany
Known for: Fast growth and high yield. Originally a dual-purpose breed (milk and meat). Often used in crossbreeding to boost size.

Brahman

Origin: India (Bos indicus)
Known for: Heat and humidity tolerance with a distinctive shoulder hump and loose skin. Commonly crossed with British breeds for hybrid vigor.

Nelore

Origin: Brazil (descended from Indian Bos indicus)
Known for: Excellent heat adaptation and parasite resistance. Lean beef with mild flavor.

Santa Gertrudis

Origin: United States, Texas (Brahman × Shorthorn cross)
Known for: First recognized beef breed developed in America. Red coat, heat-tolerant, and well-suited to Gulf Coast conditions.

Wagyu

Origin: Japan
Known for: Intense marbling and buttery texture. "Wagyu" means Japanese cattle and includes several breeds (most commonly Japanese Black in the U.S.). Higher fat content than most beef breeds.

Braford

Origin: United States (Brahman × Hereford cross)
Known for: Heat tolerance with good marbling potential. Combines Hereford's beef quality with Brahman's climate adaptability.

What This Means for Shoppers

Breed is only part of the story. How cattle are raised—pasture vs. grain, genetics within the breed, and ranch practices—affects beef quality as much as breed name.

Traceable sourcing matters. Ranches on Acre & Plate claim DNA testing, breed registrations, or named herds. Check ranch websites for verification details.

Crossbreds are common. Many ranches raise crossbred cattle (like F1 Wagyu × Angus or Brangus) to balance traits like marbling, hardiness, and climate adaptation.
