Cattle futures push higher as corn digests WASDE

Live cattle futures rallied to start the week, with cash trade firming to around $222 in the North and $226 in the South according to analysts on Monday. The rally follows a technical breakout from a declining wedge pattern earlier this month, with funds adding back long positions after reducing exposure in August.

Wholesale beef values held steady, with USDA boxed beef cutouts showing Choice at $376.08 and Select at $356.36 per hundredweight on September 15. Packers reportedly asked some producers to bring contracted cattle in early, signaling tighter near-term supply as heifer retention in slaughter data dropped below 32 percent.

For ranch-direct buyers, the firming cash market reflects tighter cattle supply heading into fall. Corn futures settled into a post-WASDE holding pattern after Friday's yield cut, which could ease feed costs for ranchers finishing cattle on grain.

USDA cuts 2026 beef production, raises imports

USDA trimmed beef production forecasts in Friday's September World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates, cutting 2026 output by 90 million pounds and 2027 by 145 million pounds. Total 2026 beef production now stands at 24.877 billion pounds, down 4.3 percent from 2025.

Despite the production cut, USDA lowered its price forecast, projecting third-quarter steers at $230 per hundredweight (down $12 from August) and fourth-quarter at $225 (down $20). The agency raised 2026 beef imports 130 million pounds to 6.262 billion pounds, with tighter domestic supply being offset by increased foreign product rather than higher cash prices.

The report signals a tightening U.S. cattle supply through 2027, which could support ranch-direct pricing as consumers look for alternatives to imported beef. Per capita beef consumption is projected to hold near 59 pounds despite the production decline.
